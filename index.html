Research Math Agents (RMA)

Long-horizon reasoning needs structured state, not just more context.

Context-Orchestrated Research Math Agents

RMA keeps a persistent research state for each problem. Before every local reasoning step, it compiles only the context that step needs, and it writes the results back so progress carries across rounds.

The problem

Why long-horizon proofs fail

Strong models often write research-level proofs that look right step by step but are incomplete as a whole. They fail by omission: the text reads as finished while a required part of the argument is still missing.

Unproved lemma

A key intermediate claim is used but never established.

Unchecked assumption

The argument depends on a condition that is never verified.

Unsupported citation

A cited result is misremembered or doesn't apply as stated.

Unverified computation

A finite or numerical claim is asserted without being checked.

Generic iterative agent transient context

  1. Round 1Proof → critique flags a missing lemma → revise
  2. Round 2New proof → new critique, mostly about presentation → revise
  3. Round 3The first critique falls out of the history window
  4. Round 4The proof reads better, but the lemma is still unproved. The gap is gone from the context, not from the proof.

RMA persistent state

  1. Round 1Critic opens Issue #17: missing lemma, P0, 4 downstream claims
  2. Round 2Solver patch fails; the attempt is saved and linked to #17
  3. Round 3Literature note on the needed technique is linked to #17
  4. Round 4Solver sees the issue, the failed attempt, and the note. The lemma is repaired, and the next critic pass rechecks it.

RMA treats unresolved proof obligations as persistent state, not as conversation context that eventually scrolls away.

Method

How RMA works

Three layers: a store that remembers, an orchestrator that decides what each call sees, and narrow operations that each do one job.

Layer 1

Persistent Research Store

One store per problem: proof revisions, open and resolved issues, prior attempts, literature notes, meeting records, a concept glossary, strategic insights, and evaluations.

Layer 2

Research Context Orchestrator

Each operation receives only the records relevant to its task, within a fixed token budget.

  1. 1RetrieveFollow the store's link graph one hop out from the records the task names.
  2. 2Rank by severity, then dependency impactP0–P3 first, then how many unresolved downstream claims depend on the affected claim. Computed deterministically from a claim-dependency graph that is rebuilt every round.
  3. 3CompileInstructions, the task, the current proof, linked records, and the operation's own recent outputs.
  4. 4Fit to budgetDrop whole records, lowest priority first (insights → concepts → literature → meetings → evaluations → issues → proof). Records are never cut mid-sentence.
  5. 5Invoke, then write backThe operation returns a typed artifact, which passes a deterministic gate before entering the store.
Layer 3

Locally scoped operations

Each operation handles one explicit research task and never sees the full store.

Critic

Runs four analyses: an LM gap critic, a deterministic structural and dependency checker, a semantic completeness judge, and a fidelity check against the problem's cited prior work.

Solver

Repairs one selected obligation at a time with an anchored patch (exact span plus replacement). Full rewrites are a gated last resort.

Literature

Retrieves results by mathematical object, dependency, and assumption, and records each technique's conditions of applicability.

Meeting

A coordinator and field-appropriate mathematician personas reconsider the global proof strategy and produce an action plan.

Revision

Applies the meeting's action plan as one coordinated global update to the proof.

Evaluator

Scores correctness, completeness, and clarity, and records the proved-leaf fraction and the issues opened and closed each round.

What actually matters?

We strengthened each mechanism step by step from its weakest working variant. The score rises at every step on all three axes, and every axis reaches the full system's 6.0.

Stronger state retention, more targeted context selection, and dependency-aware prioritization each improve performance monotonically. The two components that govern state, the store and the orchestrator, account for the largest single drops.

Failure analysis

Where RMA still fails

The two First Proof B1 problems that did not receive unanimous "correct" verdicts, described from the system's own development record.

Local repair cannot fix a wrong global strategy

Problem 3 · Markov chain with Macdonald stationary distribution · 5 of 5 experts: incorrect
  1. Early attempts repeatedly pursue the one construction the problem explicitly rules out
  2. The critic flags it correctly every time
  3. The solver has nothing local to patch, because the strategy itself is the defect
  4. After many rounds, a meeting replaces the global strategy
  5. The proof roughly doubles in length, and each critic pass closes old gaps but finds new ones deeper in

Issue discovery can be accurate claim by claim and still be the wrong lever for a defect at the level of the overall strategy. Here the growing proof also outpaced the critic's ability to re-verify it.

The critic catches an overclaim and forces a retreat

Problem 8 · Lagrangian smoothings · 2 correct, 3 inconclusive, 0 incorrect
  1. The system converges on an unconditional "yes" proof of about 1,900 lines
  2. A later critic pass finds the global claim overreached
  3. The claim is withdrawn and the question is declared open
  4. The system delivers smaller, unconditional partial results instead

An honest downgrade from "theorem" to "partial results" is neither a clean proof nor a clean error, which is consistent with the split expert verdict.

Persistent state helps preserve proof obligations, but it does not guarantee global correctness.
