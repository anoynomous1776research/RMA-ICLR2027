Abstract

Long-horizon mathematical reasoning fails less often because a model cannot produce a valid next step than because an agent fails to maintain and expose the right semantic state across many iterations. Left unmanaged, this produces research-level proofs that are locally convincing yet globally incomplete: a key lemma unproved, an assumption unchecked, a citation unsupported, or a computational claim unverified. We present Research Math Agents (RMA), an agentic framework built on a general principle – persistent, structured state combined with task-conditioned context compilation lets an agent reason more reliably over long research trajectories than undifferentiated context management – and instantiates this principle for proof development as a persistent, issue-driven process. The Research Context Orchestrator is the central state-management layer between the persistent research store and each locally scoped proof operation: it retrieves task-relevant artifacts, compiles them into a bounded context, invokes the appropriate operation, and writes the resulting proof edits, issue updates, literature notes, plans, or evaluations back to the store. This process allows RMA to identify, prioritize, and repair missing proof steps while preserving progress across rounds. Across complementary evaluation settings, RMA outperforms strong baselines. On the independently evaluated SOOHAK Challenge Hard set, it achieves a 42.5% solve rate, compared with 12.5% for its standalone Claude Opus 4.8 backbone. Under blind human-expert evaluation on First Proof, RMA obtains 8 of 10 correct solutions on B1 and 8 of 10 passing solutions on B2, surpassing GPT-5.2R, Aletheia, ProofCouncil, and other research-math agents at a cost comparable to leading agentic pipelines. RMA also achieves the strongest results on ResearchMath and Formal Conjectures, with better lemma decomposition and higher Lean 4-verified success on previously solved research problems.

Method

Research-level proofs usually fail by omission: the text reads as finished, but a required part of the argument is still missing. Repairing such a gap needs more than another critique of the current draft. It needs the unresolved claim itself, what depends on it, what has already been tried, and which literature applies. RMA therefore treats proof development as a state-management problem and organizes it into three layers.

Persistent Research Store. Each problem has one store S=(Π,I,M,L,K,H,E)\mathcal{S}=(\Pi,\mathcal{I},\mathcal{M},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{E})S=(Π,I,M,L,K,H,E). It holds proof revisions, open and resolved issues, meeting records, literature notes, a concept glossary, strategic insights, and evaluations. Each issue records the affected claim, the defect type, the missing justification, and the repair needed, so later rounds can act on it directly instead of re-deriving the diagnosis from the proof text.

Research Context Orchestrator. Four complementary critic analyses find issues: an LM gap critic, a deterministic structural and dependency checker, a semantic completeness judge, and a fidelity check against the problem’s cited prior work. Issues are ranked by severity (P0–P3) and then by dependency impact, the number of unresolved downstream claims that rest on the affected claim. Both rankings are computed deterministically from a claim-dependency graph that is rebuilt every round. For every call, the orchestrator compiles the operation’s instructions, the task, the current proof, the linked records, and the operation’s own recent outputs. It then drops whole records in ascending priority until the context fits the budget BBB, so it never cuts a record mid-sentence.

Locally scoped operations. The critic, solver, literature researcher, meeting, reviser, and evaluator each receive only their compiled observation and return a typed artifact that passes a deterministic gate before it is written back. The solver repairs one issue at a time with anchored patches. Meetings and coordinated revision handle strategy-level changes that local repairs cannot reach.

Results

We evaluate on four benchmarks that use complementary evaluators: third-party expert grading (SOOHAK), blind review by mathematicians (First Proof B1 and B2), Lean 4 kernel verification (Formal Conjectures), and an LLM-judge protocol (ResearchMath). Unless noted otherwise, RMA uses the Claude Opus 4.8 backbone.

SOOHAK Challenge Hard

SOOHAK Challenge Hard has 40 non-public, contamination-resistant problems. We submitted RMA’s API endpoint, and the benchmark organizers evaluated it under their official expert protocol. Baseline numbers are reported by the SOOHAK authors.

Formal Conjectures (Lean 4)

For Formal Conjectures, the same loop produces Lean 4 proofs. Cost, scaling, and ablations

Varying only the API budget, RMA goes from 4 passes at $153 to 8 at $1,850, with no further gain at $3,571. Varying only the number of rounds, the continuous judge score rises from 3.2 with one round to 5.3 with four and 6.0 with six. In the component ablations, the two components that govern state cause the largest drops from 6.0: reducing the persistent store to a stateless variant gives 1.8, and dumping the full state into context instead of orchestrating it gives 2.4.
